Born in Shiraz, Iran, I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Georgia, Athens, GA, USA, specializing in artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning. My dissertation focuses on developing hybrid evolutionary neural network architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with attention mechanisms and Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs). I conduct my research under the supervision of Dr. Khaled Rasheed, Professor of Computer Science and Director of the Institute for Artificial Intelligence at UGA. My academic foundation includes both Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Electrical-Telecommunication Engineering from Shiraz University, Iran, which provided the technical grounding for my interdisciplinary research in AI.

I have a diverse professional background that spans both academia and industry. I have served as a Teaching Assistant at the University of Georgia since August 2020, supporting multiple undergraduate courses and receiving a Teaching Assistant Award for excellence in instructional support. In Summer 2024, I worked as a Research and Development Scientist Intern at Roocreations Company, where I collaborated with the CEO and a university professor to design and optimize a novel experimental gradient-descent neural network architecture. Additionally, I am currently a Research Assistant in the College of Public Health, where I apply machine learning techniques to structured healthcare data to analyze post-acute care outcomes using large-scale CMS datasets.

Prior to my doctoral studies, I worked as a Design Electrical Engineer at PIDEC in Shiraz, contributing to ten to fifteen engineering projects in industrial systems, including heat tracing, lighting, communication, and surveillance. This hands-on engineering experience has strengthened my practical problem-solving abilities and enhanced my capacity to address real-world challenges through research.
